# Level design in the engine editor

Levels are scenes/maps a person can open in the engine editor and change by hand. They are built
through the engine's MCP (Godot: instancing scenes, placing nodes, GridMap/CSG; Unity: prefabs in
scenes; Unreal: actors, level instances), not generated by runtime code. **2D game
(`dimension=2d`): also read `${extensionPath}${/}skills${/}level-design/references/2d-levels.md`.** Runtime code may spawn prepared enemy, pickup and effect scenes; it
does not decide where the walls are.

## 1. Metrics before geometry

Measure the player and every enemy class in the running game first and write them into
`memory/pipeline.md` → Level metrics: eye height, capsule radius/height, walk/run speed, jump
height and distance, step height the controller climbs, mantle reach (step/vault/mantle/
climb-up/jump-grab heights, crawl and crouch clearances, fall-damage tiers: traversal skill);
for each mech class its
footprint, height, step-over height, and whether it climbs walls. Size every corridor, door,
gap and cover piece in these units (e.g. corridor ≥ 2.5 × the widest mech that must pass,
low cover just below the crouched eye height, high cover above standing eye height).

## 2. Beat chart before blockout

Write it in the level's task file, one row per space:
`space | size (m) | purpose | enemies (classes, count) | cover | landmark | light/mood | exit cue |
signature moment | intensity 1–5`. Pacing follows a sawtooth: tension, release, higher tension.
Introduce → develop → twist → conclude for each new threat or mechanic (about 5 minutes each).

Layout grammar: **arenas** (multiple cover lines, ≥ 2 flanking routes, verticality, a readable
exit) joined by **connectors** (streets, alleys, stairwells that pace the player, preview the next
space and limit what can follow) and **breathers** (low threat, pickups, story beats, a vista of
what comes next). Linear progression with local choice. Gate with locks, one-way drops or
abilities; preview landmarks through sightlines and vistas.

## 3. Blockout

- CSG or a GridMap of greybox tiles at real scale. CSG is for prototyping: bake it
  (CSG → Bake Mesh Instance / Bake Collision Shape, 4.4+) before relying on it.
- Keep each arena in its own sub-scene (`scenes/levels/<level>/arena_<n>.tscn`) so two roles can
  work on different arenas, and the level can stream arenas with threaded loading.
- Play the blockout (playtest skill): traversal time per space, sightlines, cover heights,
  enemy approach routes, where the player looks on entry. Never put art on an untested blockout.
- **Edge audit** (static, before any playtest): flood-fill the walkable cells from the spawn on
  a 0.25–0.5 m grid (down-rays for floor, step and slope limits from the character metrics), and
  at every walkable cell on the region's border cast rays outward at jump-apex height; any border
  cell whose rays hit nothing within a jump distance is an open edge (the player can leave the
  level). Fix with a barrier, a kill/respawn volume or geometry, then rerun. g2: an 18-run
  random-walk probe found one open ground edge; this static check found three gaps in minutes.
  Keep it as `tests/probes/probe_edges.gd` (acceptance `probe_base.gd`) and rerun it after every
  layout change.
- **Winnable, both ways** (static): export the level as a layout (a room graph, a top-down grid,
  or a side-view platformer grid with the controller's jump in tiles) and run procgen
  `procgen_check.py`: every goal reachable, no softlock (a pit deeper than the jump, a one-way
  drop, a key spent on the wrong door), and with `"return": true` the way back to the start.
  Hand-made levels break the same way generated ones do; the check is the same.
- **Landmark error, re-runnable:** a level whose composition is judged from a reference frame
  (the art director's RefCam) gets a probe that unprojects each landmark's world point through
  the RefCam and reports its frame position vs the target (fraction of frame), headless, JSON.
  A note "measured in the running game" cannot be rerun after the next volume change (row 95).

## 4. Encounters (example: a shooter against large walking machines)

- Push-forward combat: arenas reward moving, flanking and target choice, not hiding forever.
- Encounter test: in a working arena the player chooses targets — runs past some enemies to kill a
  priority one. If every enemy is handled the same way, the mix or the space is wrong.
- Give each mech class a role (swarming light mechs that flank, medium line mechs, heavy
  mini-bosses that zone) and spaces that play to it: light mechs need routes to flank through;
  heavies need room to telegraph and for the player to circle.
- Spawn points out of direct view with a visible or audible cue; events triggered by player
  movement, not timers.
- Show the danger, show the escape, then let the player fail fairly.
- The world remembers: destruction, decals and debris persist where they matter.

## 5. Art pass and dressing

Replace greybox with modular kit pieces and props from `assets/models/environment/`, keeping the
blockout's collision footprint. Missing assets are not the level designer's to model — list them
for the modeler and keep greybox stand-ins named `todo_<asset>`. Kits for GridMap go into a
MeshLibrary (one root scene with MeshInstance3D children, cell size matching the meshes).
Dressing must not block navigation or create snags — check with visible collision shapes on.

## 6. Structure inside the scene

```
Level (Node3D)
├── Geometry        # static walls, floors (imported -col / -convcolonly meshes, GridMap)
├── Props           # instanced prop scenes
├── Destructibles   # instanced destructible scenes
├── Navigation      # NavigationRegion3D per area (non-overlapping), per agent-size map
├── Spawns          # Marker3D spawn points, grouped per encounter
├── Encounters      # trigger Area3Ds + encounter scripts
├── Lighting        # lights, WorldEnvironment, probes, GI nodes (owned by the lighting artist)
└── Occlusion       # OccluderInstance3D for large blockers
```

## 7. Navigation

- Different agent sizes need **separate navigation maps** with meshes baked for each agent
  radius/height. Navigation *layers* are access rules (which regions a query may use), not
  sizes.
- Regions must not overlap; neighbouring regions connect when edge vertices match or lie within
  `edge_connection_margin` — use baking bounds plus `border_size` for chunks.
- Bake from collision shapes, not visual meshes (mesh parsing is slow and stalls rendering when
  re-baking at runtime). After destruction opens or blocks a route, re-bake that region
  (`bake_navigation_mesh(true)`); `NavigationObstacle3D` only carves at bake time.
- Jumps, drops and climbs use `NavigationLink3D` — the link moves nobody; the agent's code reacts
  to `link_reached` and performs the traversal.
- Wall-climbing enemies do not walk the floor navmesh on walls: author climb routes
  (`Path3D`/markers) connected to the floor mesh with links, mark climbable surfaces with a group
  and physics layer, and record both in `memory/registries.md`.

## 8. Lighting hand-off

The lighting artist owns lights and environment, but the level designer leaves room for them:
exits and landmarks the path can be lit toward, motivated light sources in the geometry (windows,
fires, neon signs), arenas open enough for an even combat light. Record intended light
motivations in the beat chart.

## 9. Readability checklist

Landmark visible from each arena entrance; the way forward is never behind the spawn view; exits
lit or framed; enemies readable against their backgrounds (the readability acceptor measures
contrast); cover reads as cover (consistent height and silhouette).

## 10. Performance

- Occlusion culling: enable it in Project Settings, bake OccluderInstance3D (it reads only
  MeshInstance3D — not CSG, MultiMesh or particles); do not move occluders at runtime.
- Visibility ranges with `visibility_parent` for HLOD on distant props; automatic mesh LOD on
  import.
- Lightmapped kit pieces need UV2 (import as "Static Lightmaps" or unwrap UV2).
- Measure in the heaviest arena (performance acceptor).
